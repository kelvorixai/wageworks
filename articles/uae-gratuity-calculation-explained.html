The Mechanics

How UAE gratuity (end-of-service benefits) is actually calculated

Gratuity isn't a flat percentage or a mystery HR number — it's a specific formula tied to basic salary and years of service, with a step change after year five and a hard cap at two years' pay.

End-of-service gratuity is a statutory benefit under UAE labour law, paid to eligible employees when their employment ends, calculated from basic salary — not gross salary, and not including housing or transport allowances. That distinction alone changes the number substantially for anyone whose allowances make up a large share of their package.

The core formula

For each of the first five years of service, gratuity accrues at 21 days of basic salary per year. From the sixth year onward, each additional year accrues at 30 days of basic salary. The two portions are calculated separately, then added together — it's not 30 days applied retroactively to the whole tenure.

The cap: two years' total salary

Total gratuity is capped at two years' worth of the employee's total salary (basic plus allowances, for the purposes of the cap itself), regardless of how the 21/30-day formula works out over a very long tenure. In practice this cap only becomes relevant after roughly two decades of continuous service at one employer, so it rarely binds for most calculations.

A worked example

Take an employee with a basic salary of AED 10,000/month who worked 8 years before their contract ended:

PeriodCalculationResult
Years 1-5 (21 days/year × 5)(10,000 ÷ 30) × 21 × 5AED 35,000
Years 6-8 (30 days/year × 3)(10,000 ÷ 30) × 30 × 3AED 30,000
Total gratuity35,000 + 30,000AED 65,000

Note the daily rate uses a 30-day month convention (basic salary ÷ 30), which is standard practice for this calculation, not a simplification.

Eligibility: the under-one-year rule

An employee who leaves before completing one full year of continuous service is generally not entitled to gratuity at all — the one-year mark is a hard eligibility threshold, not a pro-rated starting point. From one year onward, the formula above applies in full from day one of service, not just from the point eligibility was reached.

Unlimited vs. limited contracts: mostly unified now

Since the UAE's 2022 labour law reforms, all new private-sector contracts are issued as fixed-term ("limited") contracts, replacing the old unlimited-contract structure. The gratuity formula itself (21/30 days, the two-year cap) applies the same way regardless of contract type under current law — the older distinction, where resignation before a certain tenure reduced an unlimited-contract employee's gratuity, no longer applies to contracts issued under the current framework.

What gratuity does not include

Housing allowance, transport allowance, and other benefits are excluded from the 21/30-day accrual calculation itself — only basic salary counts. This is the single most common source of confusion: an employee estimating gratuity off their gross monthly pay will consistently overestimate it, sometimes substantially, if allowances make up a large share of the package.
